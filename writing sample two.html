Writing Sample #2

Cost Benefits of Outsourcing

Written by: Joshua J. Lavallais


Some of us are strong proponents of autonomy, and we opt to handle every job ourselves versus outsourcing. Yes, lawn care, house cleaning, and cooking have proven to be therapeutic for some people. But for those who are thrifty, do-it-yourself (DIY) champions, or both, there's a distaste for dependency on others. Furthermore, one’s distaste for dependency is amplified when one has a peculiar way in which one wants something done. Consequently, as some of us strive for absolute sovereignty, we sometimes overlook the cost benefits of outsourcing.


I, too, favor self-sufficiency, though sometimes outsourcing jobs to others is beneficial. OK, but what if the job you are outsourcing is achievable given your respective skill set? Still, should you hire someone? Yes! When you can, you should employ others to handle small jobs. Before disclosing my reasons for outsourcing, I’d like to reiterate a clause from the previous sentence: when you can. Said clause involves your potential budget constraints or the nature of the job. For example, if your budget does not permit outsourcing, then DIY it is. Or – if the job in question is a private matter, disallow others from intervening.


Let’s return to my reasons for outsourcing:


1. Invest in the economy.

2. Invest in yourself.


Enter the majesty of commerce.


THE MAJESTY OF COMMERCE


Outsourcing stimulates America's economy. Undoubtedly, I, along with other proud Americans, love this! Without question, an unhealthy economy yields trouble, like high unemployment rates and inflation – issues we face in current American society. I must say that outsourcing small jobs isn't the sole preventive measure to avoid an economic collapse but rather an agent to mitigate hardship in a downbeat economy.


Exactly how does outsourcing stimulate our economy? Imagine you have a job you wish not to tackle. First, all required costs to hire help won't impact your budget. Second, the job is not a private matter and thus allows for outside intervention. Great – hire someone!


OK, this is how it works:

1. All funds earned by your hiree will cover expenses such as groceries, utility bills, or other living costs.

2. Next, the merchant of the offered products and services are paid and will use those funds for business and personal intentions.


Subsequently, money that originates from your wallet will bounce or circulate multiple times within an economic system.

Circulation is splendid, but what if your hiree deposits all their earnings in savings? Still, those funds will circulate into the economy. For example – your hiree’s bank will reap benefits, as will the merchants I previously mentioned. Remember, a bank is both a business and a lending institution. Concerning the business side, a bank has operating costs. On the lending side, banks issue loans for mortgages, car notes, and personal loans.


Remember, banks lend to businesses, too. Like banks, businesses have overhead. And while some businesses fail, those that succeed help stimulate the economy:

1. Employees of successful companies receive salaries.

2. Employees then use their earnings to finance things such as car notes, mortgages, and personal loans.

3. A business's overhead pays utility costs, employee benefits, and reinvestment ventures.


So, to maintain overhead and function as a lender, banks use customer deposits as one of many ways to fund these activities. Moreover, without a healthy circulation of money, banks can't lend, customers won't spend, and stockpiles of cash lose value, becoming worth less than the paper used to print them.


The Value of Time



My time is an asset. I have declared to many people that time is valuable, so I use it wisely. Sometimes, I use my time as a tradable.


My economics professor taught me that when two parties engage in trade, both are better off, even if one or both participants have an abudance of their corresponding tradable. If mowing your lawn or changing your oil prohibits you from investing time into something of more value, then halt. I once believed in total autonomy until I learned about opportunity cost. In short, opportunity cost is a potential benefit you might lose when choosing one alternative over another.


Let’s assess the opportunity cost of personal lawn care versus devoting time towards investing in yourself. What are you giving up? What will you collect in return?


With landscaping, you receive:


1. Relaxation.

2. Exercise.

3. Savings, as you did the job yourself.


With outsourcing, you get:


1. More time.

2. With time, you participate in self-improvement activities such as meditation, learning, career advancement initiatives, or other goals.

3. The option to spend more time to spend with loved ones—priceless.


If outsourcing lawn care duty is soft on your budget and you allow outside intervention, then the best choice is evident.


Your chosen endeavor for betterment may not be a Cash Cow or a Dog, but sometimes money isn’t, nor should it be, the coveted trophy for all accomplishments. You might think you’re losing if you outsource. Not necessarily. Remember that value isn’t solely attached to money. Value also resides in experiences without price tags, e.g., good health, happy marriages, mentorship, or volunteering. After all, some of the best things in life are free, and absolute wealth is living a life of quality and fulfillment.

Thanks for reading!

Joshua J. Lavallais
