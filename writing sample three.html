Writing Sample #3

Monday Morning Motivation

Written by: Joshua J. Lavallais


Sunday is the official start of each week. But for many full-time employees, Mondays mark the beginning of a fresh seven-day stretch. Despite the newness of the pending week ahead, Monday mornings are dreaded by many. According to an article by Emily Douglas of Human Resources Director, research compiled from Zety.com disclosed that 81% of employees hate Mondays. Also, 70% of employees revealed they suffer from the Sunday scaries!


Before moving on, I’d like to pose the following questions:


1. How long has it been since you’ve experienced an unwavering anticipation of Monday morning?

2. Can you recount the last Sunday night you experienced insomnia – the kind of wakefulness derived from unbridled excitement? I’m talking about a desire for Monday equivalent to a child’s noticeable unrest on Christmas Eve. Yes – that kind of feeling.


If only every Monday morning could be this way, right? However, for some, this is reality. Some people experience this type of sensation nearly every Monday morning.


So, for the disenchanted ones, when they open their eyes and rise on Monday morning, they’re stuck in first gear. Mentally, our downtrodden subjects are stuck on prior weekend events, reluctant to face their official start of the week.


But all is not lost, as there are antidotes to Monday Morning Melancholy. Among the different antidotes are two common remedies for Monday mornings: caffeine and exercise. Rest assured, sometimes caffeine isn’t enough to help you embrace Monday morning. When caffeine fails, ensuing is a double or triple serving to cope with Monday morning melancholy. If this is you – wait – not so fast, as too much caffeine could result in jitters. Then, we have exercise, which is by far better than caffeine.


Yet, I want you to imagine this –


Let's assume you are one of many fraught with anxiety over Monday mornings. You exercise, break a sweat, and feel energized. And just maybe, you take in a little caffeine, resulting in zero jitters. You are ready and willing to slay Monday, right? Well – not quite. Despite the endorphin release from exercise and possibly caffeine, you are still in first gear.


Our scenario does not negate the power and importance of exercise, nor the sometimes-helpful modicum of caffeine. No. Instead – we should focus on why caffeine and exercise are devoid of potency against Monday Morning Melancholy. And that reason is the absence of something meaningful.



THE PURSUIT OF SOMETHING MEANINGFUL


Earlier, I said, “Despite the newness of the pending week ahead, Monday mornings are dreaded by many.” Newness alone doesn’t cause excitement when the object received is mundane. Question: Would you get excited over a new roll of paper towels? I didn’t think so. As for Monday mornings, they’re the start of a new week, but another serving of the same old thing – unless you pursue something meaningful.


Conventional advice for overcomming Monday mornings may include the following:


1. Take a vacation

2. See a movie

3. Dine at your favorite restaurant


These suggestions are purposeful, but only if they influence one to seek and achieve greatness. Such pursuit stimulates one's mind beyond everyday drudgery. Yet the idea that buttered popcorn, an all-inclusive resort, or your favorite meal could spark grandiose ideas is a bit far-fetched. I suppose anything is possible, but this is a game of chance. And pleasures such as trips, movies, and dinners are mere Band-Aids within the context of dissolving Monday morning misery.


In place of reliance on chance, let’s take a different approach – think intently about which idea or venture could add spice to your life. Something meaningful. Something that is a mountain to climb, offering new heights to pursue while enthralling you on a seemingly never-ending journey. In sequence, a new purpose refines the spirit, as something of value is within reach.


As for myself, blogging became my thing—a creative pursuit to showcase my creativity. Blogging led to an interest in web design – both skills I continue to hone with enthusiasm. In July of 2023, I took on another activity: Muay Thai kickboxing. Training in Muay Thai is everything I expected: it is difficult in some ways but rewarding when progress is evident.


Yes, I mentioned exercise as a common antidote to Monday Morning Melancholy. Like martial arts, exercise is an agent to optimize health and fitness goals. But unlike weightlifting or running, martial arts are dynamic because there is always something new to learn, and the newness of it all never fades.


As you contemplate which venture to embrace, recall your interests and personal goals. More specifically – before closing – I’d like to introduce the Japanese concept of Ikigai, which encourages people to discover their life purpose to live lives of fulfillment. Ikigai is composed of four principles:


1. what you love

2. what the world needs

3. what yields income

4. what you are good at


In my article, Cost Benefits of Outsourcing, I said value also resides in experiences without price tags, and absolute wealth is living a life of quality and fulfillment—this is true. Yet, look at the components of Ikigai. To attain skills in something you love, something the world needs, and something people will pay you for in exchange for your expertise – well, Monday mornings never looked better!



Thanks for reading!

Joshua J. Lavallais
